About IWHM

A Race That Doesn't Start at the Start Line

But a runner's journey rarely begins at the start line. It begins months earlier — on that first Sunday morning that feels too early, at the third kilometer that feels too far, in the small decision to leave the house anyway when no one is asking you to.

Three Distances, the Same Question

5K, 10K, and Half Marathon aren't rungs on a ladder you have to climb in order. They're three different ways of asking yourself the same thing: how far am I capable of going?

For some, the answer is the first five kilometers of their life. For others, it's however many Half Marathons they've already run. The weight of the question doesn't change. Neither does the courage it takes to sign up.

Racing, Without Racing Each Other

There's a podium at IWHM, and there are runners who come to win it. But between the start line and the finish, something else happens too.

Someone slows down so the runner beside her doesn't fall behind. Someone screams from the Cheering Zone for a stranger. Someone has already finished, but chooses to stay near the finish line — not for the medal, but for a friend who hasn't crossed it yet.

None of this ever shows up in the finishing times.

Continuing the Movement, Redefining the Journey

IWHM 2026 continues what was built in 2025 — and pushes itself to go further. Not just a longer distance or a bigger field, but how deeply a running event can matter to the people living it.

Because beyond the medals and the kilometers, what's most worth celebrating is the strength, confidence, and togetherness that grows along the way. That's what makes this journey impossible to stop.

Who's Running

IWHM is open to women from all backgrounds — athletes, recreational runners, running communities, and students.
